The Balkan Backpacking Route: A Budget Traveler's Dream

2026-06-27 · Destinations · Tags: destinations, The, backpackerguide

When I first told friends I was planning a backpacking trip through the Balkans, most responded with a puzzled look. "Isn't that a war zone?" or "Why not just do Western Europe?" were common reactions. But after three months of winding through rugged mountains, turquoise rivers, and medieval towns that rival any in Italy or France, I can confidently say the Balkans are the best-kept secret in European travel. Not only is it staggeringly affordable—think $30 a day for a private room and hearty meals—but it's also a region of incredible diversity, where Ottoman minarets stand beside Austro-Hungarian facades, and where every border crossing feels like stepping into a new world. This guide will show you how to craft an unforgettable Balkan backpacking route without breaking the bank.

Why the Balkans Are a Backpacker's Paradise

The Balkans—a peninsula that includes countries like Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Montenegro, Albania, North Macedonia, and Greece—offer everything a budget traveler craves: dramatic landscapes, rich history, and warm hospitality, all at a fraction of Western European prices. Unlike the crowded beaches of the Amalfi Coast or the tourist-packed streets of Prague, the Balkans remain refreshingly authentic. You can hike through Plitvice Lakes National Park in Croatia, wander the old town of Mostar with its iconic bridge, or relax on the Albanian Riviera without fighting for a spot on the sand.

What truly sets the Balkans apart, though, is the value. A dorm bed in a hostel in Belgrade might cost you $10, a filling meal of cevapi and fresh bread around $5, and a bus ride between cities often under $15. Even in the pricier spots like Dubrovnik, you can still find budget-friendly guesthouses just outside the old city walls. For backpackers, this means you can stretch your travel fund further and stay longer, truly soaking in the culture rather than rushing through.

Crafting Your Route: From Ljubljana to Athens

There's no single "right" way to traverse the Balkans, but a classic route that balances highlights with off-the-beaten-path gems starts in Slovenia's capital, Ljubljana, and winds south to Athens, Greece. This path takes you through seven countries and offers a mix of coastal beauty, mountain adventures, and urban energy. Here's a suggested itinerary that I've refined over multiple trips:

  • Ljubljana, Slovenia – A charming, green city with a castle and a riverside market. Use it as a base to explore Lake Bled, just an hour away.
  • Zagreb, Croatia – A vibrant capital with a lively café culture and the quirky Museum of Broken Relationships.
  • Plitvice Lakes, Croatia – A UNESCO World Heritage site of cascading waterfalls and boardwalk trails. Arrive early to beat the crowds.
  • Split and Hvar, Croatia – The Dalmatian coast is stunning, but skip the overpriced ferry to Hvar and instead take a day trip to the quieter island of Brač.
  • Mostar, Bosnia and Herzegovina – Famous for its Ottoman bridge and the surrounding old bazaar. Don't miss the nearby Kravica Waterfalls.
  • Sarajevo, Bosnia – A city that beautifully blends East and West, with a rich history and incredible food.
  • Belgrade, Serbia – A gritty, energetic metropolis known for its nightlife and fortress.
  • Kotor, Montenegro – A fjord-like bay with a medieval old town and hiking trails up to the fortress for panoramic views.
  • Shkodër and Tirana, Albania – Albania is a budget traveler's dream, with friendly locals and unspoiled landscapes. Tirana's colorful buildings and café scene are a must.
  • Ohrid, North Macedonia – A lakeside town with ancient churches and a relaxed vibe.
  • Meteora, Greece – Monasteries perched on towering rock pillars, a surreal sight.
  • Athens, Greece – End your trip with history, souvlaki, and the Acropolis.

This route can be done in 4-6 weeks, but if you have more time, consider adding detours to the Albanian Riviera, the Pelion Peninsula in Greece, or the wilds of Kosovo. The beauty of the Balkans is that you can easily improvise—buses are frequent, and locals are happy to recommend hidden spots.

Budget Breakdown: How Much to Expect

One of the biggest myths about the Balkans is that it's as expensive as Western Europe. In reality, your daily costs will be significantly lower, especially if you stick to local eateries and public transport. Here's a rough breakdown based on my experiences:

  • Accommodation: Hostels range from $8–15 per night in Serbia, Bosnia, and Albania; $15–25 in Croatia and Slovenia. Private rooms in guesthouses can be found for $20–30 in most places.
  • Food: Street food like burek (flaky pastry) or cevapi (grilled meat) costs $2–4. A sit-down meal at a local restaurant runs $6–10. Cooking in hostel kitchens can cut costs further.
  • Transport: Buses are the backbone of Balkan travel. A 4-hour ride might cost $10–15. Trains are less reliable but cheaper in some countries. Consider overnight buses to save on accommodation.
  • Activities: Many natural wonders are free or have minimal entrance fees (e.g., Plitvice Lakes is around $20, but worth it). Hiking is free, and museums often have student discounts.

On average, a budget-conscious backpacker can live comfortably on $30–40 per day, including a few beers and the occasional splurge. If you're really pinching pennies, you can get by on $25 in Albania or North Macedonia. Compare that to $60–80 in Italy or France, and you'll see why the Balkans are a budget traveler's dream.

Hidden Gems and Must-See Spots

While the route above covers the highlights, the Balkans are full of lesser-known treasures that reward those who venture off the main path. Here are a few of my favorites:

  • The Blue Eye, Albania – A natural spring with impossibly blue water, surrounded by forest. It's a bit out of the way, but a perfect spot for a swim and a picnic.
  • Užice, Serbia – A small town with a fascinating open-air museum of traditional life, and a great base for exploring the nearby Tara National Park.
  • Berat, Albania – Known as the "City of a Thousand Windows," this Ottoman-era town is a UNESCO site and far less crowded than Mostar.
  • Lake Ohrid, North Macedonia – Not just the town, but the entire lake is a UNESCO site. Rent a bike and cycle to the Bay of Bones, a reconstructed prehistoric settlement.
  • Kotor's Ladder – The hike up to the fortress is well-known, but continue past it to the abandoned village of Krstac for a truly eerie experience.
  • Meteora's Back Trails – Instead of the main road, take the footpath that connects the monasteries. It's quieter and offers better views.

These spots are not only beautiful but also affordable—many are free to visit. The key is to talk to locals and other travelers; they'll point you to places that aren't in any guidebook.

Practical Tips for a Smooth Journey

Traveling through the Balkans is generally safe and straightforward, but a few insider tips can make your trip even smoother:

  • Border crossings: Have your passport ready, and be prepared for occasional delays. Some borders (like between Croatia and Bosnia) can be busy in summer. Carry small change for any road taxes.
  • Currency: The euro is used in Slovenia, Croatia, Montenegro, and Greece, but Serbia uses the dinar, Bosnia the mark, Albania the lek, and North Macedonia the denar. Withdraw cash locally—ATMs are widespread, but credit cards aren't accepted everywhere.
  • Language: English is widely spoken in tourist areas, but learning a few phrases in the local language (like "hvala" for thank you in Serbian/Croatian, or "faleminderit" in Albanian) goes a long way.
  • Transportation: Bus schedules can be erratic, so always confirm times at the station. In some countries, minibuses (furgons) are faster and more frequent than big coaches. For flexibility, consider renting a car with a group of friends—it can be cheaper than you think.
  • Safety: The Balkans are safe for travelers, but be mindful of your belongings in crowded areas, especially in cities like Belgrade and Sarajevo. Also, be aware of landmines in some rural areas near former conflict zones—stick to marked trails.
  • Seasonality: The best time to visit is May-June or September-October, when the weather is pleasant and crowds are thinner. July and August are hot and busy, especially on the coast.

One more tip: don't overplan. The Balkans are a place where you might meet a fellow traveler who tells you about a hidden beach or a village festival, and you'll want to change your plans. Embrace the spontaneity—it's part of the adventure.

Conclusion

The Balkans offer a rare combination of affordability, beauty, and authenticity that's hard to find elsewhere in Europe. Whether you're hiking through Montenegro's Durmitor National Park, sipping coffee in a Sarajevo café, or watching the sunset over the Adriatic from a tiny Albanian village, you'll be rewarded with experiences that feel genuine and unscripted. As a backpacker, you'll find that your money goes further, your interactions are warmer, and your memories are richer. So pack your bags, leave the well-trodden path behind, and discover why the Balkans are truly a budget traveler's dream. Your wallet—and your soul—will thank you.
