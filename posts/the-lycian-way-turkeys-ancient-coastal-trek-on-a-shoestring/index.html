The Lycian Way: Turkey's Ancient Coastal Trek on a Shoestring

2026-07-07 · Treks · Tags: treks, backpackerguide, The

Stretching over 540 kilometers along Turkey's turquoise coast, the Lycian Way is a trek that feels like a secret whispered between ancient ruins and pine-scented ridges. It’s a path that weaves through forgotten cities, over goat trails, and past cliffs that drop into the Aegean—yet many backpackers assume it’s a luxury adventure reserved for guided tours and deep pockets. Nothing could be further from the truth. With a bit of savvy planning, a willingness to sleep in family-run pensions, and a taste for simit and çay, the Lycian Way can be one of the most affordable long-distance treks in the world. This guide will show you exactly how to walk it without breaking your budget—or your spirit.

Why the Lycian Way Deserves a Spot on Your Bucket List

The Lycian Way isn’t just a hike; it’s a journey through time. Named after the ancient Lycian civilization that once thrived here, the trail connects crumbling amphitheaters, rock-cut tombs, and medieval castles with some of the most dramatic coastal scenery in the Mediterranean. You’ll traverse beaches where sea turtles nest, climb to mountain passes with views of distant islands, and wander through villages where life has changed little in centuries. Unlike the crowded trails of the Alps or the Himalayas, the Lycian Way remains blissfully quiet—you’ll often walk for hours without seeing another soul. And because it’s a point-to-point route, you can customize your distance, skip sections, or even hike just a few days if that’s all you have. The trail is well-marked with red and white stripes, but a good map or GPS app is essential. The best part? The cost of living in Turkey is still a fraction of Western Europe, making this trek a dream for budget-conscious adventurers.

Planning Your Trek: Timing, Transport, and Trail Basics

The ideal seasons for the Lycian Way are spring (April to June) and autumn (September to November). Summer temperatures can soar above 40°C, making the exposed limestone sections brutal, while winter brings rain and snow to the higher passes. Spring offers wildflowers and cooler days, while autumn provides warm seas and a golden light that photographers adore. To reach the trailhead, fly into Antalya or Dalaman, then take a local bus to the start at Ölüdeniz or Hisarönü. The trail is typically walked from west to east, ending near Antalya, but you can start anywhere. Public buses (dolmuş) run frequently along the coast, so you can easily jump on or off the trail if you need a rest day or want to skip a less interesting section. For navigation, download the offline maps from Maps.me or use a dedicated Lycian Way app—cell service is spotty, but the trail is well-marked. You don’t need a permit, and wild camping is generally tolerated, though it’s always polite to ask locals first.

Budgeting for the Lycian Way: What It Really Costs

Here’s the good news: you can hike the entire Lycian Way for under $30 a day if you’re smart. The biggest expenses are accommodation and food, but both can be kept low. A typical budget breakdown looks like this:

  • Accommodation: Pensions in villages cost between 150-300 Turkish Lira (about $5-10 USD) per night, including breakfast. Camping is free if you bring a tent, but you’ll need to carry water and find flat ground.
  • Food: Local markets and bakeries are your best friends. A loaf of bread, cheese, olives, and fresh fruit cost less than $3. Eating at a lokanta (small restaurant) for a hearty meal of grilled meat, rice, and salad runs about $5-7.
  • Transport: Buses between trail towns are cheap—a 2-hour ride costs around $3-5. If you need to skip a section, it’s affordable.
  • Miscellaneous: Water is free from village fountains, but you’ll need to buy bottled water in remote stretches. A 1.5L bottle costs about $0.50. Add in the occasional çay stop (a glass of Turkish tea is about $0.30) and you’re set.

To save even more, travel with a partner to split costs, and always ask for the “backpacker” rate at pensions—many owners will knock off a few lira if you’re staying multiple nights. Also, consider hiking in a group of four or five; you can share taxis to trailheads and cook communal meals.

Where to Sleep: Pensions, Camping, and Wild Bivouacs

One of the joys of the Lycian Way is the variety of sleeping options. In larger towns like Kaş, Kalkan, and Fethiye, you’ll find hostels and budget pensions with dorm beds for as little as $8. In smaller villages, family-run pensions are the norm—they often include a home-cooked dinner for an extra $5, which is a steal. If you’re camping, look for designated campsites near beaches or in pine forests; many charge a small fee (around $3) for a pitch, but wild camping is legal in most areas as long as you’re not on private property. A word of caution: the terrain is rocky and often steep, so finding a flat, tent-friendly spot can be tricky. Always carry a lightweight tent and a sleeping pad that can handle uneven ground. For the truly adventurous, some sections have stone shepherd huts (called yayla) that are open for emergency shelter—just be respectful and leave them as you found them.

Fueling Up: Local Food and Water Strategies

Turkish cuisine is a backpacker’s dream—fresh, flavorful, and cheap. Start your day with a traditional breakfast of bread, white cheese, olives, tomatoes, and eggs, which most pensions include. For lunch, grab a simit (sesame-covered bread ring) from a street vendor, or make your own sandwiches with market ingredients. Dinner is the main event: seek out a lokanta where you can point at the day’s dishes—usually a choice of stewed meats, vegetables, and rice. Don’t miss the chance to try gözleme (savory stuffed flatbread) at roadside stands; it’s a filling and inexpensive snack. For water, carry a reusable bottle and refill at village fountains—most are safe to drink, but if you’re unsure, use a purification tablet or a filter. In the dry summer months, water sources can be scarce, so always carry at least 2 liters and plan your campsites near known springs. A great tip: ask locals for the nearest “çeşme” (fountain) before you set out—they’ll point you to the best spots.

Essential Gear and Packing Tips for the Lycian Way

You don’t need fancy equipment to conquer the Lycian Way, but you do need to be smart. The trail is rugged, with sharp limestone that can tear up shoes and skin. Invest in a pair of sturdy trail runners or lightweight hiking boots with good grip—you’ll be grateful on the rocky descents. A 40-50 liter backpack is plenty, as you’ll be carrying your own tent, sleeping bag, and food. Here’s a checklist of must-haves:

  • Footwear: Broken-in boots or trail runners, plus a pair of sandals for evenings.
  • Clothing: Lightweight, quick-dry layers. A sun hat, sunglasses, and a buff are essential. Even in summer, nights can be cool, so bring a fleece or light down jacket.
  • Sleeping: A 3-season tent, a sleeping bag rated to 10°C (50°F), and a sleeping pad that can handle rocky ground.
  • Navigation: Offline maps on your phone, a physical map as backup, and a compass (though the trail is well-marked).
  • Water: A 2-liter hydration bladder or bottles, plus a purification method.
  • Sun protection: High-SPF sunscreen, lip balm, and a wide-brimmed hat.
  • First aid: Blister plasters, antiseptic, painkillers, and any personal meds.

Pack light—you’ll be carrying everything, and the heat will make every extra gram feel like a brick. Leave behind the camping stove if you’re staying in pensions; you can always eat out. But if you plan to wild camp, a small stove is worth its weight for morning coffee and hot meals. And don’t forget a headlamp—the trail can be tricky after dark, and you’ll want to navigate to your campsite safely.

Conclusion

The Lycian Way is more than a trek—it’s a lesson in slow travel, a celebration of ancient history, and a testament to the fact that adventure doesn’t have to cost a fortune. By embracing local culture, sleeping under the stars, and eating like a Turk, you can walk this stunning coastline for less than the price of a night in a European hostel. So pack your bags, lace up your boots, and set off on a journey that will leave you with memories—and a few extra lira in your pocket. The trail is waiting, and it’s more accessible than you ever imagined.
