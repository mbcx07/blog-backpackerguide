The Făgăraș Ridge: Romania's High Alpine Trekking on a Shoestring

2026-06-18 · Destinations · Tags: The, destinations, backpackerguide

When most backpackers think of Eastern Europe, they picture medieval towns, cheap beer, and the occasional vampire legend. But tucked away in the heart of Romania lies a mountain range that rivals the Alps in drama and beauty, yet costs a fraction of the price. The Făgăraș Mountains, part of the Southern Carpathians, offer some of the most spectacular high-altitude trekking in Europe—think jagged peaks, glacial lakes, and vast wilderness—all without the crowds or the hefty price tag. For the budget-conscious adventurer, this is the ultimate destination: a place where you can hike for days, sleep under the stars, and spend less than you would on a single night in a Swiss hut. Here’s everything you need to know to tackle the Făgăraș Ridge on a shoestring.

Why the Făgăraș Mountains?

The Făgăraș range stretches over 70 kilometers, with more than a dozen peaks exceeding 2,500 meters. Moldoveanu, the highest point in Romania at 2,544 meters, is the crown jewel, but the real magic lies in the ridge itself—a continuous, knife-edge traverse that offers panoramic views of the Carpathians and the Transylvanian plateau below. Unlike the crowded trails of the Alps or the Dolomites, the Făgăraș remain blissfully quiet, even in peak summer. You’ll share the path with a handful of Romanian hikers, the occasional shepherd, and a lot of marmots and chamois. The terrain is raw and untamed: steep ascents, exposed ridges, and glacial cirques filled with turquoise lakes. It’s a true wilderness experience, and because the infrastructure is minimal, the costs are too. No cable cars, no luxury huts, no overpriced restaurants—just you, your backpack, and the mountains.

Getting There and Getting Around

Your journey begins in either Bucharest or Sibiu, both well-connected by train and bus. From Bucharest, take a train to Pitești, then a bus to Curtea de Argeș, the southern gateway to the mountains. Alternatively, from Sibiu, hop on a bus to Bâlea Lake—the northern access point and the start of the classic ridge walk. Bâlea Lake is also reachable by the Transfăgărășan Highway, one of the most scenic roads in the world, but public transport only runs during summer (June–September). If you’re flexible, hitchhiking is common and safe in Romania; drivers often pick up backpackers heading to the mountains. Once you’re on the trail, you’ll rely on your own two feet, but you can also use the occasional mountain hut (cabana) for a meal or a bed, though they’re basic and cheap. For getting between trailheads, local buses and shared taxis are affordable—expect to pay around 10–15 lei (€2–3) for short hops.

The Classic Ridge Walk: Bâlea Lake to Podragu

The most iconic section of the Făgăraș is the traverse from Bâlea Lake to Podragu Lake, a two-to-three-day trek that covers the highest peaks, including Moldoveanu and Negoiu. The trail is well-marked with red stripes (the Romanian marking system), but it’s demanding: expect 1,000–1,500 meters of elevation gain per day, with some exposed sections that require scrambling. Start at Bâlea Lake (2,034m), where you can stock up on water and snacks at the cabana. From there, follow the ridge south, passing over Vârful Bâlea (2,507m) and then descending to the Șaua Caprei saddle. Camp here or push on to the Cabana Podragu, a rustic hut that offers dorm beds and hot meals for around 50 lei (€10) per night. The next day, tackle Moldoveanu—a steep but non-technical climb—then descend to Podragu Lake, a stunning glacial cirque. Many hikers continue to the village of Victoria, but you can also loop back via the Șaua Podragu pass. The scenery is relentless: jagged limestone cliffs, alpine meadows, and the occasional ibex. Bring a tent if you want to save money—wild camping is legal in Romania (except in national parks, but the Făgăraș are not strictly regulated), and there are plenty of flat spots near the lakes.

Budget Breakdown: What It Really Costs

Here’s the good news: the Făgăraș are one of the cheapest high-mountain treks in Europe. A week on the trail will set you back less than €200, including transport from Bucharest. Here’s a rough breakdown:

  • Transport: Train from Bucharest to Pitești (€5), bus to Curtea de Argeș (€3), and a shared taxi to the trailhead (€5). Return from the north via bus from Bâlea Lake to Sibiu (€7). Total: ~€20.
  • Accommodation: Mountain huts cost €8–12 per night for a dorm bed. Camping is free if you bring your own tent. Mix it up: stay in a hut on rainy nights, camp on clear ones. Average: €5–10 per night.
  • Food: Buy staples (pasta, rice, canned goods, bread, cheese) from supermarkets in Sibiu or Curtea de Argeș. A week’s worth of food costs about €25. Huts sell hot meals for €5–8, but they’re a splurge.
  • Permits and fees: None. The Făgăraș are open to all, no park fees.
  • Miscellaneous: Maps, bus tickets, and the occasional beer at a cabana—€20.

Compare that to the Alps, where a single night in a hut can cost €30–50, and you’ll see why the Făgăraș are a budget backpacker’s dream.

Money-Saving Tips and Tricks

To keep your costs even lower, follow these insider strategies. First, wild camp as much as possible. The Făgăraș have no strict camping bans, and the best spots are near the glacial lakes—just make sure to leave no trace. Second, cook your own meals. A lightweight stove and a pot are worth their weight in gold; you’ll save a fortune compared to eating at huts. Third, resupply in the towns before you start. Sibiu and Curtea de Argeș have excellent markets where you can buy local cheese, cured meats, and fresh bread for a fraction of supermarket prices. Fourth, travel in a group. Splitting the cost of shared taxis and buying food in bulk can cut your expenses by 20–30%. Fifth, avoid the peak season (August) if you can. July and September are just as beautiful, but huts are cheaper and less crowded. Finally, don’t be afraid to hitchhike. Romanians are incredibly friendly, and a ride from the trailhead to the nearest town is often free—just be patient and smile.

Conclusion

The Făgăraș Mountains are a hidden gem for budget backpackers who crave real adventure without the tourist crowds or the alpine price tag. With their dramatic ridges, pristine lakes, and a sense of remoteness that’s hard to find in Western Europe, they offer an experience that’s both challenging and deeply rewarding. And because the infrastructure is minimal, your money goes further—allowing you to spend more time on the trail and less time worrying about your wallet. So pack your tent, lace up your boots, and head to Romania. The Făgăraș are waiting, and they won’t cost you a fortune.
