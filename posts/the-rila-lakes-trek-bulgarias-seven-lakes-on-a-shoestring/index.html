The Rila Lakes Trek: Bulgaria's Seven Lakes on a Shoestring

2026-05-17 · Treks · Tags: backpackerguide, treks, The

When most backpackers think of Europe's great alpine treks, their minds drift to the Swiss Alps, the Dolomites, or the Tour du Mont Blanc. But tucked away in the southwestern corner of Bulgaria lies a high-altitude wonder that rivals them all in beauty—and undercuts them in cost by a landslide. The Rila Lakes trek, centered around the famous Seven Rila Lakes, offers jagged peaks, crystal-clear glacial lakes, and sweeping alpine meadows, all for a fraction of the price you'd pay in Western Europe. For budget-conscious adventurers who crave genuine wilderness without the tourist crowds or the premium price tag, this is a trek that deserves a spot on your radar. In this guide, I'll show you how to conquer the Rila Lakes circuit on a shoestring, without sacrificing an ounce of the experience.

Why the Rila Lakes Trek?

The Seven Rila Lakes are a group of glacial lakes nestled in the Rila Mountains, the highest mountain range in the Balkans. Each lake has a name that reflects its shape or character—the Tear, the Eye, the Kidney, the Twin, the Trefoil, the Fish, and the Lower Lake. The trek connecting them is a classic day hike, but you can easily stretch it into a multi-day adventure by exploring the surrounding peaks and valleys. What makes this trek so special is its accessibility: you can reach the trailhead from Sofia, Bulgaria's capital, in under two hours, and the entire region is remarkably affordable. Unlike the crowded trails of the Alps, you'll often have the mountains to yourself, especially if you go in June or September. The scenery is dramatic—steep cliffs, emerald lakes, and panoramic views of Mount Musala, the highest peak in the Balkans. And the best part? You can do the whole thing on a budget of less than €50 per day, including accommodation, food, and transport.

Getting to the Trailhead: From Sofia to the Lakes

Your journey begins in Sofia, a vibrant city that's already a backpacker favorite for its low prices and lively hostel scene. From Sofia Central Bus Station, you can catch a direct bus to the town of Sapareva Banya, which is the most common starting point for the Rila Lakes trek. The bus ride takes about 1.5 hours and costs around 8 leva (€4). From Sapareva Banya, you'll need to get to the Panichishte resort, which is the base of the chairlift that takes you up to the trailhead. A local minibus (marshrutka) runs regularly and costs about 3 leva (€1.50). Alternatively, you can hike from Sapareva Banya to Panichishte, but that adds a few extra hours and a steep climb—not ideal if you're short on time.

Once at Panichishte, you have two options: take the chairlift up to the Rila Lakes hut (about 12 leva one-way, or 18 leva return) or hike the 3-4 hour trail that ascends through dense pine forests. If you're on a strict budget, hiking up is the way to go—it saves you money and gets you warmed up for the day ahead. But if you're short on time or want to save your energy for the lakes themselves, the chairlift is a worthwhile splurge. The chairlift operates from 9 AM to 5 PM, and during peak season (July-August), it can get busy, so aim to start early.

The Trek Itself: Route, Difficulty, and Highlights

The classic Rila Lakes trek is a circular route that starts at the Rila Lakes hut (2,100m) and takes you past all seven lakes, with a total distance of about 12 kilometers and an elevation gain of around 600 meters. The trail is well-marked with red-and-white stripes, but you'll also encounter plenty of other hikers to follow. The difficulty is moderate—there are some steep sections, particularly between the Eye and the Kidney, but nothing that requires technical skills. Most people complete the circuit in 4-6 hours, depending on how often you stop to soak in the views.

The route begins with a gentle climb from the hut to the Lower Lake, which is the largest of the seven. From there, you'll ascend to the Fish Lake, then the Trefoil, and eventually reach the highest point—the Tear, which sits at 2,535 meters. This is where you'll get the most dramatic views, with the lakes scattered below like a string of sapphires. The descent takes you past the Eye and the Kidney, which are the most photogenic of the lakes, before looping back to the hut. If you're feeling adventurous, you can detour to the summit of Mount Otovishki (2,636m) for an even higher perspective—it's a 45-minute scramble that rewards you with a 360-degree panorama.

For those who want to extend the trek into a multi-day adventure, the Rila Mountains offer endless possibilities. You can connect to the Rila Monastery, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, via a 2-3 day trek that crosses the mountain range. Or you can climb Mount Musala, the highest peak in the Balkans, which is a challenging but rewarding day hike from the nearby Musala hut. The Rila Lakes trek is also a great introduction to the region, so you can easily add on these extra days without blowing your budget.

Budget Breakdown: What It Really Costs

One of the biggest draws of the Rila Lakes trek is its affordability. Here's a rough breakdown of what you can expect to spend per day, based on my own experience and current prices:

  • Accommodation: If you're staying in Sofia, a bed in a hostel dorm costs between 15-25 leva (€8-13) per night. In the mountains, you can stay at the Rila Lakes hut for about 20 leva (€10) per night, but you'll need to book in advance during peak season. The hut offers basic dormitory-style rooms, shared bathrooms, and a communal kitchen.
  • Food: Groceries in Bulgaria are incredibly cheap. A loaf of bread costs less than 1 leva, a kilogram of tomatoes around 2 leva, and a block of local cheese about 5 leva. If you cook your own meals, you can easily eat for under 10 leva (€5) per day. Eating out at a restaurant in Sofia will cost around 15-20 leva (€8-10) for a full meal, but in the mountains, the hut serves simple, hearty meals for around 10-15 leva.
  • Transport: The bus from Sofia to Sapareva Banya is 8 leva, the marshrutka to Panichishte is 3 leva, and the chairlift is 12 leva one-way. If you're hiking up, you save that 12 leva. For a two-day trip, you can expect to spend around 30 leva (€15) on transport.
  • Entrance fees and permits: There are no entrance fees for the Rila Lakes trek itself. However, if you plan to visit the Rila Monastery, there's a small donation box (2-3 leva) and a parking fee if you drive.

All told, you can easily complete a two-day, one-night trip to the Rila Lakes for under 100 leva (€50), including accommodation, food, and transport. That's a fraction of what you'd pay for a similar trek in the Alps, where a single night in a mountain hut can cost upwards of €30 and a bus ticket can set you back €20.

Where to Stay and Eat: Huts, Hostels, and Local Flavors

The Rila Lakes hut is the main accommodation hub at the trailhead. It's a classic Bulgarian mountain hut—rustic, cozy, and functional. Dorm beds are basic but clean, and the communal areas are perfect for swapping stories with fellow trekkers. The hut serves breakfast, lunch, and dinner, with traditional dishes like banitsa (cheese-filled pastry), shopska salad, and bean stew. Prices are reasonable, and the portions are generous. If you're on a strict budget, you can bring your own food and use the hut's kitchen to cook, but note that there's a small fee for using the stove and pots (around 2 leva).

Back in Sofia, you'll find a thriving hostel scene with plenty of budget options. I recommend staying in the central area, near Vitosha Boulevard, where you can walk to the bus station and enjoy the city's nightlife. Many hostels offer free breakfast, which is a great way to save money. For a truly local experience, try a family-run guesthouse in Sapareva Banya, where you can soak in the thermal pools after your trek for a few extra leva—a perfect way to soothe sore muscles.

When it comes to food, don't miss the chance to try Bulgarian specialties. In the mountains, look for mehana (traditional taverns) that serve grilled meats, kebapche, and tarator (cold cucumber soup). These are not only delicious but also incredibly cheap. A meal at a local mehana will set you back around 10-15 leva, and you'll leave feeling stuffed. For a truly budget-friendly option, visit the local bakery (banitsa shop) in any town and grab a slice of warm banitsa for under 2 leva—it's the perfect trail snack.

Tips for Making the Most of Your Rila Lakes Trek on a Shoestring

To truly maximize your budget and your experience, here are some insider tips I've picked up from multiple visits to the Rila Mountains:

  • Go in the shoulder season: June and September offer the best weather and the fewest crowds. Accommodation prices drop, and you'll have the trails to yourself. Avoid July and August if you can, as the hut fills up quickly and the chairlift queues can be long.
  • Hike up instead of taking the chairlift: The 4-hour ascent from Panichishte to the Rila Lakes hut is a workout, but it saves you 12 leva and gives you a sense of accomplishment. Plus, you'll pass through beautiful forest and might spot deer or wild boar.
  • Cook your own meals: If you're staying at the hut, buy groceries in Sofia before you leave. A simple pasta dish or instant soup will cost a fraction of what you'd pay at the hut's restaurant. Just remember to pack light—you'll be carrying everything on your back.
  • Share costs with other trekkers: The Rila Lakes trek is popular, and you'll likely meet other backpackers at the hut. Split the cost of a taxi or a shared marshrutka, or team up to buy a bigger pot of food. It's a great way to save money and make friends.
  • Bring a reusable water bottle: The lakes are glacial, but the water is not safe to drink directly. Instead, fill up at the hut's tap, which has potable water. This saves you from buying bottled water at inflated prices.
  • Consider a multi-day trek: Instead of just a day trip, plan a 3-4 day trek that includes the Rila Lakes, Mount Musala, and the Rila Monastery. This spreads your transport costs over more days and gives you a deeper experience of the region. You can stay at different huts along the way, each offering a unique perspective.

One more thing: don't underestimate the weather. Even in summer, the Rila Mountains can be cold and windy, especially at higher elevations. Pack layers, a waterproof jacket, and sturdy hiking boots. A hat and gloves are essential, even in July. And always check the weather forecast before you set out—storms can roll in quickly, and the trails become slippery.

Conclusion

The Rila Lakes trek is a hidden gem in the heart of the Balkans, offering world-class alpine scenery at a price that's almost too good to be true. From the moment you step off the bus in Sapareva Banya to the final panoramic view from the Tear, this trek delivers an unforgettable adventure without breaking the bank. Whether you're a seasoned backpacker or a first-time trekker, the Rila Lakes will reward you with stunning landscapes, a rich cultural experience, and the kind of budget-friendly travel that BackpackerGuide is all about. So pack your bags, lace up your boots, and head to Bulgaria—the Seven Lakes are waiting, and they won't cost you a fortune. Happy trails!
