The Rila Lakes Circuit: Bulgaria's High-Altitude Gem on a Shoestring

2026-05-18 · Treks · Tags: The, treks, backpackerguide

Forget the crowded, permit-quota madness of the Alps or the over-touristed paths of Patagonia. There is a place in Eastern Europe where jagged granite peaks plunge into glacial lakes the color of sapphire, where you can hike for days without seeing another soul, and where your biggest daily expense might be a 2 lev (about $1.10) cup of coffee at a mountain hut. Welcome to the Rila Mountains of Bulgaria, home to the Seven Rila Lakes and a network of trails that offer some of the best value trekking on the continent. This isn't just a walk in a national park; it's a raw, high-altitude adventure that feels a world away from the polished trails of Western Europe. As a budget backpacker, I've chased summits from the Andes to the Himalayas, and I can say with confidence: the Rila Lakes Circuit is the single most underrated trek I've ever laced up my boots for. It delivers the drama of the high mountains, the charm of rustic mountain huts, and a cultural immersion that leaves you feeling like you've discovered a secret. This guide is your all-in-one resource for conquering this stunning circuit without breaking the bank.

Why the Rila Lakes Circuit Deserves a Spot on Your Bucket List

The Seven Rila Lakes are a group of glacial lakes nestled high in Bulgaria's Rila National Park, the largest national park in the country. They are a natural wonder, each lake sitting at a different altitude and connected by a series of trails that form a spectacular circuit. The trek is a perfect blend of challenge and accessibility. You can do it as a long, strenuous day hike, or—as I highly recommend—as a two or three-day backpacking trip, staying overnight in the iconic mountain huts that dot the landscape. The scenery is the main draw: stark, rocky ridgelines, alpine meadows bursting with wildflowers in the summer, and the impossibly clear, cold lakes that reflect the sky like mirrors. But it's the feeling of remoteness that truly sets it apart. While the area around the chairlift can get busy with day-trippers, a short walk from the main hub transports you into a wilderness that feels far more vast and untamed than its size suggests. For the budget traveler, this is a dream. Park entry is free, the mountain huts are a fraction of the cost of Alpine refuges, and the local transport is incredibly cheap. You get a world-class trekking experience for the price of a night out in a Western European capital.

Getting There and Getting Oriented: Your Gateway to the High Mountains

Your journey begins in the town of Sapareva Banya, a small spa town known for its hot springs, or the larger city of Dupnitsa. Both are easily reachable from Sofia, Bulgaria's capital. From Sofia, you can catch a direct bus to Sapareva Banya (around 2 hours, costing roughly 8-10 lev). The bus station in Sofia is the central one (Centralna Avtogara), and buses run several times a day. From Sapareva Banya, you'll need to take a local bus or a taxi to the Rila Lakes lift station (Pionerska). The local bus is infrequent, so a taxi is often the most reliable option, costing around 20-25 lev. The lift itself is a classic, open-air chairlift that takes you from the valley floor (around 1,500m) up to the trailhead at about 2,100m. A one-way ticket is about 15 lev, and a return is 25 lev. While you can hike up from the valley, the lift saves you a grueling 600-meter ascent and gives you a bird's-eye view of the forest below. It's an experience in itself, and the cost is a bargain. Once you're at the top, you'll find a well-marked trailhead, a small restaurant, and the start of the main path leading towards the lakes.

The Trek Itself: A Two-Day Itinerary for the Full Experience

To truly appreciate the Rila Lakes, I recommend a two-day, one-night trek. This allows you to hike the entire circuit at a relaxed pace, soak in the views, and experience the magical sunset and sunrise from a high-altitude hut. Here’s how to break it down.

Day 1: The Ascent and the High Lakes. From the top of the chairlift, follow the well-worn path towards the first lake. The trail is rocky but clear, and you'll quickly pass the Lower Lake (Dolnoto ezero). The path then climbs steadily, passing the Fish Lake (Ribnoto ezero) and the Trefoil Lake (Trite listnika). This is a great place to stop for a snack and take in the views. The real highlight of the day is the climb to the highest lake, the Salzata (Tear), which sits in a dramatic cirque beneath the peak of Otovishki Vrah. The final push is a bit of a scramble, but the reward is immense. From here, you'll descend slightly to the Kidney Lake (Bubreka) and then make your way to the hut. The most popular and well-equipped hut is the Rila Lakes Hut (Hijha Sedemte Ezera), which sits right on the shore of the largest lake, Okoto (The Eye). This is your base for the night. The hut offers dormitory-style beds, a cozy common room with a fireplace, and a kitchen serving hearty Bulgarian fare like banitsa (cheese pastry) and shopska salad. A bed in the dorm costs around 25-30 lev per night, and a hot meal is around 10-15 lev. It's a social hub, and you'll meet trekkers from all over the world.

Day 2: The High Ridge and the Descent. Wake up early to catch the sunrise over the lakes. It's a moment you won't forget. After a hearty breakfast, you'll tackle the most spectacular part of the circuit: the climb to the ridge above the lakes. The trail from the hut heads steeply up the grassy slopes to the saddle between the peaks. This is the highest point of the trek, offering panoramic views of the entire lake system and the surrounding Rila massif. From the saddle, you can optionally bag a peak like Otovishki Vrah (2,696m) for an even more rewarding view. The descent from the saddle is on the other side, taking you down a rocky path that passes the Twin Lake (Bliznaka) and the last of the seven, the Lake of the Eye (Okoto), which you'll see from above. The trail loops back down to the chairlift, but instead of taking it, I highly recommend walking the final stretch down through the pine forest. It's a beautiful, shaded descent that takes about an hour and gives you a different perspective on the mountain. You'll end up back at the lift station, tired but exhilarated, with a profound sense of accomplishment.

Budget Breakdown: What It Really Costs to Trek the Rila Lakes

One of the best things about this trek is how incredibly affordable it is. Here's a realistic breakdown of costs for a two-day trip, assuming you're traveling from Sofia.

  • Transport (Sofia to Sapareva Banya and back): ~20 lev (buses)
  • Taxi to the lift station: ~25 lev (split between 2-3 people)
  • Chairlift (one-way): 15 lev
  • Accommodation in the hut (one night): ~30 lev
  • Food (dinner, breakfast, snacks): ~25 lev
  • Total: ~115 lev (approximately $60 USD)

That's less than the cost of a single night in a hostel in a major Western European city. You can easily shave off even more by bringing your own food, camping (though it's not officially allowed in the park, so check the rules), or skipping the chairlift and hiking up from the valley. The point is, this is a world-class trek that is accessible to almost any budget.

Essential Gear and Pro Tips for the Rila Lakes

While the trek is not technically difficult, it is high-altitude and the weather can change in an instant. Being prepared is key to having a great time.

  • Footwear: A pair of sturdy, broken-in hiking boots with good ankle support is non-negotiable. The terrain is rocky and uneven.
  • Layers: Even in summer, temperatures can drop to near freezing at night. Pack a base layer, a warm mid-layer (fleece or down), and a waterproof/windproof outer shell. A warm hat and gloves are essential.
  • Sun Protection: The sun at altitude is intense. Bring high-SPF sunscreen, sunglasses, and a hat.
  • Water: There are streams and lakes, but it's best to treat the water. Bring a filter or purification tablets. The water from the mountain springs is generally safe, but treating it is a good habit.
  • Navigation: The trails are well-marked with colored paint (red and white stripes), but a map or a GPS app on your phone is a wise backup. Download an offline map of the area before you go.
  • Cash: The mountain huts and the lift station often don't accept credit cards. Bring enough Bulgarian lev in cash to cover your expenses.
  • Book Your Hut in Advance: During the peak season (July-August), the Rila Lakes Hut can get very busy. It's a good idea to call ahead or book through a local agency to guarantee a bed. You can also find information on the Bulgarian Tourist Union website.

One of the best pieces of advice I can give you is to be flexible with your schedule. The weather in the mountains is fickle. If the forecast looks bad, don't be afraid to delay your trek by a day. The views are infinitely better when the skies are clear, and the experience is much safer.

Beyond the Lakes: Extending Your Rila Adventure

If you have more time, the Rila Mountains offer a vast network of trails that can be combined with the Seven Lakes circuit to create a longer, more immersive trek. You can hike from the Rila Lakes Hut over to the famous Rila Monastery, a UNESCO World Heritage site, in a challenging but rewarding two-day trek. The monastery is a stunning example of Bulgarian Revival architecture, with its striking black-and-white striped arches and beautiful frescoes. This extension adds a cultural dimension to your adventure that is truly unforgettable. Alternatively, you can head towards the Musala Peak, the highest point in the Balkans at 2,925 meters. The climb from the lakes is a long but non-technical hike that rewards you with panoramic views that stretch all the way to Greece and Romania on a clear day. These extensions are perfect for the backpacker who wants to get off the beaten path and truly explore the wild heart of Bulgaria.

Conclusion

The Rila Lakes Circuit is more than just a trek; it's a revelation. It proves that you don't need a massive budget or a permit lottery to experience the raw beauty of the high mountains. It's a place where the air is crisp, the water is pure, and the landscapes are so dramatic they feel almost otherworldly. For the budget-conscious backpacker, it's a goldmine—a chance to test your limits, connect with nature, and immerse yourself in a culture that is warm, welcoming, and deeply connected to its mountains. So, pack your bags, lace up your boots, and head to Bulgaria. The Seven Rila Lakes are waiting, and they are calling your name. This is the kind of adventure that reminds you why you travel in the first place.
