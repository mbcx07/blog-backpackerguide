The Accursed Mountains: Trekking the Albanian Alps on a Backpacker's Budget

2026-07-20 · Destinations · Tags: destinations, backpackerguide, The

There’s a corner of Europe where the mountains are so jagged and remote that they were given a name that sounds like a warning. The Accursed Mountains, or the Bjeshkët e Nemuna, stretch across the borderlands of Albania, Montenegro, and Kosovo, and for decades, this region was as closed off as it was beautiful. But now, the secret is out. This is one of the last true wildernesses on the continent, a place where you can hike for hours without seeing another soul, where guesthouses cost a fraction of what you’d pay in the Swiss Alps, and where the trails are as raw and untamed as the name suggests. For the budget-conscious backpacker, this isn’t just a trek; it’s a revelation. It’s a place where your money goes further, your legs work harder, and your memories last a lifetime. This is your guide to conquering the Accursed Mountains without breaking the bank.

Why the Accursed Mountains? A Budget Trekker's Paradise

Forget the crowded, permit-controlled trails of the Himalayas or the Andes. The Accursed Mountains offer a different kind of adventure, one that feels genuinely undiscovered. The primary draw is the raw, dramatic scenery. Think of towering limestone peaks that shoot straight up from green valleys, deep gorges carved by glacial melt, and crystal-clear rivers that are perfect for a post-hike dip. It’s a landscape that feels prehistoric, and the best part? You can experience it for a fraction of the cost of other European trekking destinations.

Your money goes a long way here. A night in a traditional guesthouse, complete with a hearty, home-cooked dinner and breakfast, will set you back around €25-30. A beer is a couple of euros. A local guide, if you choose to hire one, is a fraction of the cost of one in the Alps. This is a place where you can stretch your travel budget significantly while enjoying a world-class trekking experience. The infrastructure is growing, but it’s still wonderfully simple. You’re not walking into a tourist trap; you’re walking into a living, breathing mountain community that is just beginning to share its home with the world.

Getting There: The Journey to Theth and Valbona

The adventure begins before you even hit the trail. The gateway to the Albanian side of the Accursed Mountains is the city of Shkodër, a vibrant, bike-friendly town with a beautiful lakeside setting and a fascinating history. From Shkodër, you have two main options to reach the trailheads, and both are an experience in themselves.

The most popular route is to take a shared minibus (furgon) to the village of Theth. The journey is a white-knuckle ride along a winding, partially paved road that clings to the side of the Cem River canyon. It’s slow, bumpy, and utterly breathtaking. The furgon typically leaves in the morning when it’s full, and the ride takes around 2.5 to 3 hours. The cost is minimal, usually around €5-7. The alternative is to head to Valbona, which is a slightly longer and equally scenic drive. You can also take a public bus to the town of Koplik and then catch a furgon, but the direct service from Shkodër is the most straightforward.

For the truly adventurous, there’s a more epic way in: the ferry across Lake Koman. This is not just a transfer; it’s a journey that many consider a highlight of the region. The ferry weaves its way through a dramatic, fjord-like gorge, with mountains rising sheer from the water. It’s a stunning, multi-hour ride that connects the town of Koman to Fierza, from where you can catch a furgon to Valbona. It costs a few euros and is an unforgettable experience. Whichever way you choose, the journey is part of the adventure, setting the tone for the raw beauty that awaits.

The Classic Trek: Theth to Valbona Pass

This is the trek that put the Accursed Mountains on the map. The route from Theth to Valbona is a classic point-to-point hike that crosses the Valbona Pass at an altitude of around 1,800 meters. It’s a challenging but achievable day hike for most people with a reasonable level of fitness. The trail is well-marked with red and white paint, but it’s a serious mountain path, not a gentle stroll.

The hike begins in Theth, a village of stone houses and a famous, solitary church. You’ll start by following the river valley, passing through fields and small farms before the trail begins to climb. The ascent is steady and can be steep in places, but the views open up dramatically as you gain height. You’ll pass the Grunas Waterfall, a stunning cascade that’s worth a short detour. The final push to the pass is a series of switchbacks that will test your lungs, but the reward is immense. From the top, you’re greeted with a panoramic vista of jagged peaks and the deep green valley of Valbona below.

The descent into Valbona is long and can be tough on the knees, but it’s a beautiful walk through alpine meadows and forests. The whole trek takes between 6 and 8 hours, depending on your pace and how many times you stop to take photos (and you will stop a lot). It’s a day that will push you, but it’s also a day you’ll never forget. The sense of accomplishment when you finally see the guesthouses of Valbona in the distance is immense.

Beyond the Pass: Extending Your Adventure

While the Theth-Valbona trek is the most famous, the Accursed Mountains offer a wealth of other trails for those who want to go deeper. If you have more time, you can easily spend a week or more exploring this network of paths. The region is a true paradise for multi-day trekking, and you can link up villages and passes to create a longer loop.

One popular extension is to continue from Valbona to the village of Çerem, a quieter and even more remote settlement. From there, you can attempt the higher, more challenging pass to the Rrogam valley, which is a true wilderness experience. Another option is to base yourself in Theth and take day hikes to the Blue Eye (Syri i Kalter), a stunning natural spring with impossibly blue water, or to the remote village of Nderlysa. For the experienced and well-equipped, the trek from Valbona over the border into Montenegro to the town of Plav is a multi-day epic that takes you through some of the most remote terrain in the Balkans.

Here are a few key things to keep in mind for extending your trek:

  • Maps and Navigation: The trails are generally well-marked, but a good offline map app (like Maps.me or AllTrails) is essential, as phone signal is non-existent in the mountains.
  • Accommodation: Guesthouses are found in all the main villages, but in the more remote areas, you may need to stay in a mountain hut or camp. Always ask locals about the next available lodging.
  • Food and Water: You can refill your water bottle from the many streams and rivers, but it’s wise to carry a filter or purification tablets. Guesthouses will provide packed lunches if you ask the night before.
  • Pack Light: You’ll be carrying everything on your back, so every gram counts. A 40-50 liter backpack is more than enough for a multi-day trek.

Where to Stay: Guesthouses, Homestays, and Hostels

Accommodation in the Accursed Mountains is part of the charm. Forget sterile hotels; here, you stay in family-run guesthouses (known as bujtina) that offer a warm welcome, a comfortable bed, and incredible home-cooked food. This is the heart of the local economy, and your money goes directly to the families who call these mountains home.

In Theth and Valbona, you’ll find a growing number of guesthouses, many of which have been built in the traditional stone-and-wood style. A typical stay includes a bed in a simple, clean room (often with a shared bathroom) and three meals a day. Dinner is a feast of local specialties: lamb or veal cooked in a traditional way, fresh salads, homemade bread, and raki, the local firewater. Breakfast is usually a spread of eggs, cheese, jam, and strong coffee. The cost is remarkably low, usually around €25-30 per person per night for full board. It’s an incredible value.

For the truly budget-conscious, there are also a few more basic hostels and guesthouses that offer just a bed and breakfast. In Shkodër, you’ll find a great selection of hostels that are perfect for meeting other travelers and organizing your trek. The hospitality is a huge part of the experience. You’ll be treated like a guest, not a customer, and you’ll often find yourself sitting around the dinner table with the family, sharing stories and learning about their way of life.

Essential Gear and Practical Tips for the Trail

While the Accursed Mountains are accessible, they are still a serious mountain environment. Being prepared is key to having a safe and enjoyable trek. You don’t need top-of-the-line expedition gear, but you do need the right basics. The weather can change quickly, even in summer, so layering is your best friend.

Your essential kit should include sturdy, broken-in hiking boots, a waterproof and windproof jacket, and a warm mid-layer (like a fleece or light down jacket) for the cooler altitudes. Sun protection is crucial, as the sun is intense at high elevations. A hat, sunglasses, and sunscreen are non-negotiable. You’ll also want a good headlamp, a reusable water bottle or hydration bladder, and a basic first-aid kit. Trekking poles are highly recommended, especially for the long descent into Valbona, as they will save your knees.

Here are a few more practical tips to ensure your trek goes smoothly:

  • Cash is King: There are no ATMs in the mountains. Bring enough cash in euros (or Albanian lek) to cover your accommodation, food, and any furgon rides.
  • Learn a Few Words: While many younger people speak some English, learning a few phrases in Albanian (like “Faleminderit” for thank you and “Përshëndetje” for hello) goes a long way.
  • Start Early: The trails can get hot in the afternoon, and afternoon thunderstorms are common in the summer. Starting your hike at 7 or 8 AM is the best way to avoid the heat and the crowds.
  • Respect the Environment: This is a pristine wilderness. Pack out all of your trash and leave no trace. The mountains are a fragile ecosystem, and it’s up to us to keep them beautiful.

Conclusion

The Accursed Mountains are a misnomer. There is nothing cursed about this place; it is, in fact, a blessing for the adventurous backpacker. It’s a destination that offers a rare combination of raw, untamed beauty, genuine cultural immersion, and incredible value for money. In a world where so many places feel over-discovered and over-priced, the Albanian Alps feel like a final frontier. The trek over the Valbona Pass is a rite of passage, a challenging but rewarding journey that will leave you with a profound sense of accomplishment and a deep connection to the landscape. So, pack your bags, lace up your boots, and head to this corner of Europe before the rest of the world catches on. Your wallet, your legs, and your soul will thank you.
