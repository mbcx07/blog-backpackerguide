The Laugavegur Trail: Iceland's Otherworldly Trek on a Shoestring

2026-05-20 · Destinations · Tags: destinations, backpackerguide

Iceland has a reputation for being one of the most expensive countries on Earth, and for good reason—a simple beer can cost you $10, and a night in a Reykjavik hostel might set you back more than a hotel room in Bangkok. But tucked away in the island's rugged interior lies a trek that defies the price tag: the Laugavegur Trail. This 55-kilometer (34-mile) route winds through a surreal landscape of rainbow-colored rhyolite mountains, steaming geothermal vents, black volcanic deserts, and emerald valleys. It's a world-class hiking experience that, with a little savvy planning, can be done on a budget that won't make your wallet weep. In this guide, I'll show you exactly how to tackle the Laugavegur Trail without breaking the bank, from getting there to sleeping under the stars.

The Laugavegur Trail: An Overview

The Laugavegur Trail is Iceland's most famous multi-day hike, running from the geothermal wonderland of Landmannalaugar in the north to the lush valley of Þórsmörk in the south. The trail is typically hiked over 4 to 5 days, covering 55 kilometers with a total elevation gain of about 1,000 meters. It's not a technical trek—there are no ropes or climbing required—but it does demand good fitness and a tolerance for unpredictable weather. The trail is open from late June to early September, with July and August being the most popular months. The landscape is nothing short of otherworldly: you'll pass through obsidian fields, cross glacial rivers, and soak in natural hot springs along the way. The best part? The trail is entirely free to hike—no permits or entry fees required. Your only costs are getting there, sleeping, and eating.

Getting to the Trailhead on a Budget

The biggest hurdle for budget travelers is reaching the trailheads. Landmannalaugar, the starting point, is about 180 kilometers from Reykjavik, and the road is only accessible by high-clearance 4x4 vehicles. The cheapest way to get there is by public bus. The Icelandic bus company Reykjavik Excursions operates a daily service from Reykjavik to Landmannalaugar during the summer, costing around 9,900 ISK (about $70) one way. That might sound steep, but it's the most reliable option. If you're really pinching pennies, consider hitchhiking—Iceland is generally safe, and many travelers have successfully hitched to the trailhead. However, the road is rough and traffic is sparse, so you'll need patience and a flexible schedule. Another budget hack is to book a bus pass that includes both the start and end of the trail. For example, you can take the bus from Reykjavik to Landmannalaugar, hike to Þórsmörk, and then catch a bus back to Reykjavik. A round-trip ticket with a stopover in Þórsmörk costs about 19,800 ISK ($140), which is still cheaper than renting a 4x4 (which can easily cost $200+ per day). If you're traveling with a group, splitting a rental might be worth it, but for solo backpackers, the bus is your best bet.

Budget Breakdown: Costs and How to Save

Once you're on the trail, your main expenses are accommodation and food. The Laugavegur Trail has a network of mountain huts operated by Ferðafélag Íslands (Iceland Touring Association). These huts offer dormitory-style beds, cooking facilities, and sometimes a small shop. A night in a hut costs around 7,500 ISK ($55) per person, which is not cheap. To save money, you can camp instead. Camping is allowed only at designated campsites near the huts, and the fee is about 2,500 ISK ($18) per person per night. That's a significant saving, but you'll need to bring your own tent and sleeping gear. If you're on a tight budget, camping is the way to go. You can also save on food by bringing your own dehydrated meals and snacks from Reykjavik. Supermarkets like Bónus and Krónan have reasonable prices for basics like pasta, oats, and nuts. Plan to cook your own meals on a portable stove—hut kitchens are available for campers too, but you'll need to bring your own cookware. A typical daily food budget on the trail can be as low as 1,500 ISK ($11) if you're smart. Add in the cost of a bus pass and camping fees, and you can complete the entire trek for under $200, excluding your flight to Iceland. That's a steal for a world-class adventure.

What to Pack for the Laugavegur Trail

Packing for Iceland is all about layering and waterproofing. The weather can change from sunshine to snow in minutes, so you need to be prepared. The good news is that you don't need to buy expensive gear—you can rent most of it in Reykjavik. Several outdoor shops, like Reykjavik Outfitters and Icelandic Mountain Guides, offer rental packages for tents, sleeping bags, stoves, and even waterproof jackets. Renting a full camping kit costs around 5,000 ISK ($35) per day, which is cheaper than buying if you're only hiking for a week. If you're bringing your own gear, focus on these essentials: a sturdy pair of waterproof hiking boots (the trail has many river crossings), a waterproof jacket and pants, a warm mid-layer (fleece or down), a hat and gloves, and a reliable headlamp. You'll also need a good backpack (40-60 liters) and a sleeping bag rated for at least 0°C (32°F). Don't forget sunscreen and sunglasses—the sun reflects off the snow and obsidian, and you can get burned even on cloudy days. For cooking, a lightweight stove and a small pot are essential. And always carry a water filter or purification tablets—the streams are generally safe, but it's better to be cautious.

Trail Highlights and Must-See Spots

The Laugavegur Trail is a visual feast from start to finish. Here are the highlights you absolutely can't miss:

  • Landmannalaugar's Hot Springs: Before you even start hiking, take a dip in the natural geothermal pool near the trailhead. It's free and the perfect way to relax before the trek.
  • Rainbow Mountains: The rhyolite hills around Landmannalaugar are a kaleidoscope of red, yellow, green, and blue. The best views are from the first day's climb up to the Brennisteinsalda volcano.
  • Hrafntinnusker: This area is a geothermal wonderland with steaming vents and bubbling mud pools. It's like walking on another planet.
  • Alftavatn Lake: After a long day of hiking, you'll descend to this serene lake surrounded by black sand and green hills. The campsite here is a great place to rest.
  • Emstrur and the Black Desert: The landscape turns stark and volcanic, with vast expanses of black pumice and the dramatic Mælifell volcano in the distance.
  • Þórsmörk Valley: The trail ends in this lush, birch-covered valley, a stark contrast to the barren highlands. It's a perfect place to celebrate your achievement.

Take your time to soak in these sights—the trail is not a race. Many hikers spend an extra day at Þórsmörk to explore the surrounding glaciers and canyons.

Practical Tips for a Successful Trek

To make your Laugavegur trek smooth and budget-friendly, keep these tips in mind:

  • Book your bus and campsites in advance: During peak season, the buses and campsites fill up quickly. Reserve your bus seat online and, if you plan to camp, you can pay on arrival, but it's wise to have a backup plan.
  • Check the weather forecast daily: The Icelandic Meteorological Office provides reliable forecasts. Be prepared to adjust your schedule if a storm is coming.
  • Cross rivers carefully: There are several unbridged river crossings. Use a trekking pole for balance and always unbuckle your backpack strap so you can escape if you fall.
  • Pack light but smart: Every extra kilo hurts. Weigh your pack before you leave and leave behind anything you don't absolutely need.
  • Respect the environment: The highlands are fragile. Stay on marked trails, camp only in designated areas, and pack out all your trash.
  • Consider hiking south to north: Most people hike from Landmannalaugar to Þórsmörk, but going the other way can be less crowded and gives you a different perspective.

With these tips, you'll be well-prepared to tackle the Laugavegur Trail without blowing your budget.

Conclusion

The Laugavegur Trail is proof that Iceland's wild beauty doesn't have to come with a wild price tag. By camping instead of staying in huts, cooking your own meals, and using public transport, you can experience one of the world's most extraordinary treks for a fraction of what most tourists spend. The memories of walking through rainbow mountains, soaking in hot springs, and sleeping under the midnight sun will stay with you forever—and they're priceless. So pack your tent, lace up your boots, and get ready for an adventure that proves the best things in life are free (or at least, very cheap). Happy trails!
